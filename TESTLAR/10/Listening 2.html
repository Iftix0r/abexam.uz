Part four. You'll hear part of a history lecture about the translation of the language spoken by ancient Mayans who lived in Central America. First, you have some time to look at questions 31 to 40. now listen carefully and answer questions 31 to 40. When the Spanish arrived in Central America in the 1500s, they were surprised by the variety of Indian cultures there. One of the most interesting of the cultures, the Maya, had partially collapsed hundreds of years before. There were Mayan ruins everywhere, and they showed evidence of advanced engineering and a sophisticated tradition of writing.

The Spanish thought that this written language of the Mayans, which had survived, 31could be a threat to the dominance of the Spanish language in the region(threat — ispan tili hukmronligiga tahdid), and so they tried to destroy all traces of it. Luckily, three books written in Mayan survived the destruction and were rediscovered in the 18th and 19th century in Europe. Each of the books contained a vast collection of ancient Mayan writings. All of these books were basically handbooks to be used by the priests.

But for a long time, no one could read them. In the 19th and early 20th century, investigators managed to decode some numbers and some parts of the text which 32seemed to be on the subject of astronomy(astronomy — matnning astronomiyaga oid qismlari), but there were still large parts of the text they couldn't understand. Then, in the 1950s and 1960s, there were some breakthroughs. An amateur Mayan scholar called Heinrich Berlin, working in Mexico, decoded the first Mayan glyphs, which is the name given to the symbols that make up the Mayan script. He showed that these glyphs, in fact, 33represented famous cities(cities — mashhur shaharlarni bildirgan) that had once been a part of the Mayan Empire. He was then able to match these symbols with spoken words, because the name for somewhere famous doesn't usually change much over the centuries.

So, by studying modern-day Mayan and listening to speakers of the evolved language, Berlin discovered how to say these symbols. Next to enter the picture was Yuri Knorozov, a Soviet linguistics expert. He discovered that many Mayan glyphs actually played a phonetic role in the text. That means 34they stood for a sound, not just a single word as previously thought(sound — so'z emas, tovushni bildirgan). So, two phonetic symbols together could, in fact, be one word, not two separate words.

The door was open to crack the code. Beginning in the 1980s, several younger academics took a new approach. Unlike their forebears, who studied Mayans from a distance, this new generation immersed themselves by living among the Central American indigenous people. These young researchers came to have a deeper understanding of Mayan art, and also 35a greater appreciation of the culture of the Mayans(culture — mayyalar madaniyati), both past and present, as well as of their religion. All this meant that there was a dramatic improvement in the quality and quantity of translation. The young academics discovered that many of the great events of Mayan history were recorded. In particular, 36many of the manuscripts described successes when kings fought wars with one another(wars — qirollarning urushlardagi g'alabalari). But if the text was formal, the writing was not.

The Mayan scribes who carved and painted the glyphs were often trying to be creative. So, there simply wasn't a standard system that scholars could refer to. But once the young academics 37got familiar with the style used by a particular Mayan writer(style — har bir yozuvchining uslubi), steady translations became possible. But this type of translation takes a lot of time. Help was needed. Starting in 2003, one prominent scholar, David Stuart, looked for an online way for scholars and amateurs to post new inscriptions, refine translations, and discuss the subtleties of Mayan language. So, 38he started a blog that evolved into what is now the Maya Decipherment Project(blog — blog ochgan).

Scholars in remote areas, who were often cut out of earlier research because of logistics, have become eager translation partners. 39Comments can be posted from anywhere in the world at high speed(speed — fikrlar yuqori tezlikda). It's estimated that there are more than 30 translators in 15 different countries currently at work studying and translating Maya script through the Maya Decipherment Project. Text by text, they are giving the modern world a better understanding of the lost Mayan world.

The direction of this new understanding is that Mayans were not necessarily peaceful people 40who were wholly preoccupied with ceremony(ceremony — faqat marosimlar bilan band deb hisoblangan). This had been the general conclusion when scholarship was limited to the handbooks for priests that survived the Spanish. Thanks in part to the Maya Decipherment Project, new scholarship shows that the Mayans were interested in, and wrote about, the same topics as other ancient societies.

That is the end of part four. You now have one minute to check your answers to part four.
